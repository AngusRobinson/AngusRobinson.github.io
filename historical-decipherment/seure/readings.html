Letter from the Commander de Seure to Monsieur du Fresne, Secretary of State under King Henry II, while Seure was his ambassador in Portugal.

My lord, I shall not write you a long letter, since you will be able to see from the one I am writing to the king what is happening here. I wrote to you recently through a man named Antoine Galuan, a Portuguese courier, and shortly before that I had done the same, rejoicing greatly at the honour you have received from His Majesty of succeeding the late Monsieur de Marchaumont, my good lord and friend. Galuan then made a good deal of difficulty about returning [to France]. Nevertheless I believe [that he] would have gone on as far as you. I know that, at the time [when he] left, our neighbours regarded the [peace] as concluded. I have had no news at all from you since the return of [Jeannoy], just as I have none yet from the king. It is true that I have indeed heard that a letter has been sent to me through someone who has not yet [arrived]; I await him with great devotion, and to hear your good news and that of my lord. I await with great devotion some word about what you are doing. Nevertheless I still have to assess it, [given] the manner

dont noz voysins parlent de la retraicte des mansulmans s’ilz disent vérité la chose sera autrement que ne l’ay espéré quoy qu’il en soit ce me sera plaisir d’entendre la résolucion car nous commenceons à approcher du temps que [vostre] amy aye achevé ses deux ans

in which our neighbours speak of the withdrawal of the Muslims. If they are telling the truth, things will turn out otherwise than I had hoped. Be that as it may, I shall be glad to hear the outcome, for we are beginning to approach the time when [your] friend will have completed his two years.

nous avons tenu icy la paix pour faicte je ne sçay ce qu’il en sera qui my faisoit encores avoir plus de doubte je ne veulx plus demeurer icy guières longuement et au pis aller n’y veux que achever mes deux ans je suis en nécessité et ay eu tout plein dettes cest esté

We have regarded the peace here as settled; I do not know how it will turn out, which made me still more doubtful. I do not wish to remain here very much longer, and at worst I want only to complete my two years. I am in need and ran up a great many debts this summer.

Letter no. 40

FrenchEnglish

Lettre du mesme Commandeur de Seure au Roy Henry second, estant pour lors son ambassadeur en Portugal.

Letter from the same Commander de Seure to King Henry II, while he was his ambassador in Portugal.

Manuscript page

Sire, Je vous ay escript le douziesme de ce moys par voye de Lyon et Bourdeaulx la grande joye que noz voisins de Séville faisoient de l’arrivée de la flotte du Pérou, laquelle y arriva dès la fin d’octobre, et la somme d’argent registrée qu’elle avoit apportée. Mais pourautant que ces voyes de vous escripre sont aussi incertaines, et que ne sçay bonnement si ce que vous ay escript au passé par diverses dépesches est allé jusques à vous, Je n’ay voulu faillir en redire ung mot à vostre majesté par ceste dépesche, laquelle va par voye de Lyon, affin qu’elle puisse estre advertie de tout ce qui s’offre en cest endroict.

Sire, I wrote to you on the twelfth of this month by way of Lyon and Bordeaux of the great joy among our neighbours in Seville at the arrival of the fleet from Peru, which arrived there at the end of October, and of the registered money it had brought. But because these routes for writing to you are so uncertain, and I do not really know whether what I have written in my various earlier dispatches has reached you, I did not wish to fail to repeat a word about it to Your Majesty in this dispatch, which goes by way of Lyon, so that you may be informed of everything that occurs here.

Ladicte flotte estoit de vingt deux navires dont les quatre estoient armez en guerre, et le demeurant estoient toutes sortes de navires marchans. Lesdictz quatre navires ont apporté huict cens mille escuz pour le Roy Philippes, qui est du revenu de ses droictz, de ces autres ung million d’argent registré pour les particuliers ou marchans, et aultres quatre vingtz mille pour les deffunctz, et pour troys cens mille escuz de cochenille et d’estoffe, et oultre ce ont desbarqué en sauluete es Isles des Assores, où par leur bon heure ilz ne trouverent plus les navires qui estoient allez à leur escorte, parce qu’ilz s’estoient retirez à Caliz désespérez de l’avoir pour ceste année, près d’ung million d’or, dont la pluspart appartient à ce que l’on dict au Visroy du Pérou, qui est tout ce qu’il en a peu venir à ma cognoissance. L’on m’escript que oultre ces sommes qui sont registrées, dont les officiers du Roy Philippe se sont saisiz, il y en avoit encores une bonne somme, laquelle ne l’estoit, que les marchans ont faict cacher en secret et au desceu des officiers des Indes parmy les sucres et autres sortes marchandises, laquelle peult à peu près valloir quasi autant que la registrée. Les officiers de Séville ont faict une merveilleuse dilligence là dessus.

The fleet consisted of twenty-two ships: four were armed for war and the rest were merchant ships of every kind. Those four ships brought eight hundred thousand écus for King Philip, from the revenue of his rights; of the others, a million in registered silver for private persons or merchants; another eighty thousand for the deceased; and three hundred thousand écus' worth of cochineal and fabrics. Besides this they unloaded safely in the Azores, where by their good luck they no longer found the ships that had gone out to escort them (for these had withdrawn to Cádiz, despairing of meeting it this year), nearly a million in gold, most of which is said to belong to the Viceroy of Peru. That is all that has come to my knowledge. I am told that, beyond these registered sums, which King Philip's officers have seized, there was still a good sum which was not [registered], which the merchants had hidden in secret, without the knowledge of the officers of the Indies, among the sugar and other kinds of merchandise; it may be worth very nearly as much as the registered money. The officers of Seville have been remarkably diligent about it.

si est ce que jusques a présent il n’en ont peu attrapper que deux cens mil escupz qui estoient à ung homme d’église lesquelz ont confisquez la royne de portugal a permis et accordé au roy philippes de pouvoir envoyer en l’isle de la tercera navires armez et officiers pour recouvrer les argent qui y a esté lais en passant et pussance de chastier et ordonner là dessus à son plaisir que les gens de bien de ce pays trouvent bien estrange comme à la vérité il est car pour riens les deffunctz roys de portugal ne l’eussent permis ilz y ont envoyé à toute dilligence dieu veille qu’ilz ne le recouvrent comme j’estime qu’ilz ne feront bien aisément

Nevertheless, up to the present they have been able to get hold of only two hundred thousand écus belonging to a churchman, which they have confiscated. The Queen of Portugal has permitted and agreed that King Philip may send armed ships and officers to the island of Terceira to recover the money left there in passing, with power to punish and make arrangements concerning it as he pleases. The honourable people of this country find this very strange, as indeed it is, for the late kings of Portugal would never have allowed it on any account. They have sent there with all diligence. God grant that they do not recover it, as I think they will not do so very easily.

Another letter from the same Commander de Seure to the said king. 1558.

Sire, Je vous escripviz par mes dernières lettres du xxvme d’octobre, que Je donnay à ung Courrier Portugais nommé Anthoine Galuan, le décez de la Royne Marie et la peyne en quoy estoient noz voisins pour le peu d’espérance qui leur restoit que la flotte qu’ilz attendoient du Pérou deust venir ceste année, à cause que don Alvaro de Bassan, qui l’avoit attendue es Isles des Assores l’espace de deux mois, s’en estoit retourné sans y avoir eu nulles nouvelles. Depuis ladicte flotte est arrivée à Séville dès le xije du mois passé, ce que Je n’ay peu escrire plustost à vostre majesté pour n’avoir eu la commoditté d’envoyer les lettres, et aussi que J’attendoys de sçavoir particulièrement la vérité de toute la somme qu’elle a apportée, laquelle se monte à deux millions d’or en argent, sçavoir huict cens mille escuz pour le Roy Philippes, et ung million pour les particuliers, et cent quatre vingtz mille pour les deffunctz qui ont leurs héritiers pardeçà. Et oultre ce la valeur de troys cens mille escuz en cochenille et aliofre qui sont perles de semence.

Sire, by my last letters of 25 October, which I gave to a Portuguese courier named Antoine Galuan, I wrote to you of the death of Queen Mary and of the distress of our neighbours at the little hope left to them that the fleet they were expecting from Peru would come this year, because Don Álvaro de Bazán, who had waited for it in the Azores for two months, had come back without any news of it. Since then the fleet has arrived at Seville, on the 12th of last month. I could not write this to Your Majesty sooner, because I had no means of sending letters, and also because I was waiting to learn exactly the whole sum it has brought. It amounts to two million in gold, in silver: namely eight hundred thousand écus for King Philip, a million for private persons, and another one hundred and eighty thousand for the deceased whose heirs are here. Besides this there is three hundred thousand écus' worth of cochineal and aliofre, which are seed pearls.

De quoy l’Espaigne a faict une merveilleuse feste, comme de chose dont ilz commençoient desjà à désespérer pour ceste année, et aussi pourautant que toute l’espérance et Recours du Roy Philippe, comme autresfoys Je vous ay escript, est désormais fondé là dessus. Ilz estoient en tout vingt deux navires, dont il y en avoit quatre de guerre qui ont amené avecques eulx deux navires de voz subiectz qu’ilz prindrent au port de la Havana, qui est en l’Isle de Cuba à l’opposite de la Florida, où ilz estoient attendant ladicte flotte, que si la bon heure eust voulu qu’elle ne feust point venue acompaignee de navires de guerre comme ilz pensoient, ou bien que lesdictz subiectz (qui estoient de Dieppe et de la Rochelle) eussent eu encores troys navires avecques eulx, Il n’y a point de faulte, ainsi comme J’ay entendu de quelque vingt mesmes qui y estoient, qu’ilz eussent tout emmené en France, et vous eussent faict pour ung coup ung trèsgrand et notable service.

Spain has made a marvellous celebration of it, as of a thing they were already beginning to despair of for this year, and also because all King Philip's hope and resource, as I have written to you before, now rests on it. There were twenty-two ships in all, four of them warships, which brought with them two ships of your subjects that they had taken in the port of Havana, which is on the island of Cuba opposite Florida, where they were lying in wait for the fleet. If luck had willed that the fleet had not come escorted by warships, as they expected, or if the said subjects (who were from Dieppe and La Rochelle) had had three more ships with them, there is no doubt at all, as I have heard from some twenty of the very men who were there, that they would have carried it all off to France and done you, at one stroke, a very great and notable service.

et par là sire vostre magestu peult assez évidemment congnoistre combien suivant ce que je vous en ay autresfoys escrit je seroyz aisé d’oster à vostre ennemy ce grant soullaigement qu’il a de ce monde de delà ou pour mieulx dires tout le nerf et tout le moyen qu’il a désormais de maintenir la guerre cotre vous

From this, Sire, Your Majesty can clearly see how—following what I have previously written to you—I would be glad to deprive your enemy of this great advantage he draws from the New World: or, more precisely, all the sinews and resources he now has to sustain the war against you.

Et par là, Sire, vostre majesté peult assez évidemment cognoistre combien suyuant ce que je vous en ay aultresfoys escript, Il seroyt aisé d’oster à vostre ennemy ce grant soullaigement quil a de ce monde de delà, ou pour myeulx dire tout le nerf et tout le moyen quil a désormais de maintenir la guerre contre vous. Ladicte flotte, oultre la susdicte somme quelle a apportée à Séville, avoit aussi desbarqué es Isles des Assores une grande quantité dargent qui est à plusieurs particuliers, et se monte bien à huit ou neuf cens mil escus, dont la pluspart appartient à celluy qui est maintenant Visroy au Pérou, qui lavoit faict desbarquer es Isles de peur que sil fust venu jusques à Séville les officiers du Roy Philippes ne sen feussent saisiz, comme ilz avoient accoustumé de faire les ans passez. Mais à ce que je voy cela ne luy servira pas beaucoup. Car la Royne de Portugal a accordé et consenty aux susdictz officiers et ministres du Roy Philippes que ces argens soient enlevez et portez en Espaigne. Et sur ce a envoyé mandemens fort rigoreux esdictes Isles pour les leur faire delivrer, ne voullant poinct permettre que quelque somme dargent qui vienne du Pérou, tant petite soit elle, se puisse desbarquer en ce Royaulme. Qui est chose que beaucoup de gens de bien de ce pays dicy ont trouvé bien estrange, et laquelle le feu Roy de Portugal, quelque alliance quil y eust entre eulx, neust jamais permise. Mais il me semble, Sire, que vous luy pouvez bien pardonner celle cy, puisque ce nest pas la première chose où elle sest monstrée bien fort passionnée pour le Roy despaigne.

From this, Sire, Your Majesty can see quite plainly how easy it would be, as I have written to you before, to take from your enemy the great relief he draws from that world over there, or rather the whole sinew and means he now has of keeping up the war against you. The said fleet, besides the sum mentioned above which it brought to Seville, had also unloaded in the Azores a large quantity of money belonging to several private persons. It amounts to fully eight or nine hundred thousand écus. Most of it belongs to the present viceroy of Peru, who had it unloaded in the islands for fear that, if it went as far as Seville, King Philip’s officers would seize it, as they had been accustomed to do in previous years. But from what I can see, this will not help him much. For the Queen of Portugal has agreed with the aforesaid officers and ministers of King Philip that this money should be taken away and carried to Spain. She has therefore sent very strict orders to the islands to make it be delivered to them. She does not wish to allow any sum coming from Peru, however small, to be unloaded in this kingdom. Many honourable people here have found this very strange. The late King of Portugal would never have allowed it, whatever alliance there may have been between them. But it seems to me, Sire, that you can readily forgive her this instance, since it is not the first matter in which she has shown herself strongly partial to the King of Spain.

Car jay entendu que par la dernière flotte des hourques qui partit dicy à la my octobre pour aller en Flandres, elle luy avoit advance une bonne somme dargent quelle avoit tirée des coffres du petit Roy son nepveu.

For I have heard that, by the last fleet of hulks which left here in mid-October for Flanders, she had advanced him a considerable sum of money taken from the coffers of the young king, her grandson.

Et peu avant cela en partira dicy une aultre de vingt hourques chargées despiceries pour Flandres, qui vauldroient bien la peine que ceulx de Dieppe ou de Sainct Malo y entendissent. Car elles vauldra infiniment. Et si ny aura que deux meschans navires de guerre a laccompaigner, et ceulx sont mal armez de toutes choses. Celle qui partit dicy en octobre est encores de présent en Galice attendant meilleur temps quelle na eu. Il sen est perdu quelques navires qui estoient de grande valleur. Les Turcqs nont point assiégé Oran, où noz voisins avoient envoyé deux mil hommes, que ilz pensoient quilz deussent faire.

Shortly before that, another fleet of twenty hulks loaded with spices will leave here for Flanders. It would be well worth the while of the men of Dieppe or Saint-Malo to concern themselves with it, for it will be worth an immense amount. There will be only two wretched warships to accompany it, and those are poorly armed and equipped in every respect. The fleet which left here in October is still at present in Galicia, waiting for better weather than it has had. Some ships of great value have been lost. The Turks have not besieged Oran, where our neighbours had sent two thousand men, as they thought [the Turks] would do.

Ilz debvoient huict cens mil escus aux foires de Medina, quilz avoient faict retarder pour navoir moyen de les fournir que après larrivée de la flotte. Lesquelz ilz ont jà payez, et depuis trouvé nouveau crédict.

They owed eight hundred thousand écus at the fairs of Medina. They had delayed payment because they had no means of providing the money until after the fleet’s arrival. They have already paid those sums and have since obtained fresh credit.

Ces jours passez ung navire normand print ung petit navire de guerre de la Royne qui lalla, à ce que lon dict, assaillir pour le prendre. Dont elle a esté en grand collère, et faict retenir les navires de voz subjectz qui estoient par son Royaume. Mais à la fin je luy ay si bien remonstré le tort que avoit son navire quelle les a relaschez.

A few days ago a Norman ship captured a small warship of the queen which, it is said, had attacked it in order to take it. She was very angry at this and had the ships of your subjects then in her kingdom detained. But in the end I explained so clearly to her the wrong committed by her own ship that she released them.
